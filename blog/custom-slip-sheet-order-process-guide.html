Custom Slip Sheet Order Process: From Spec to Delivery

Corrugatedslipsheets Editorial • September 2026

Understanding Custom Slip Sheet Requirements

Ordering custom corrugated slip sheets requires clear communication of dimensional tolerances, material specifications, and performance criteria. Most procurement teams begin with pallet dimensions and load characteristics, then work backward to determine optimal sheet sizing and flute configuration. For standard 48×40-inch GMA pallets, slip sheets typically measure 48×45 inches to provide adequate edge coverage during push-pull handling operations.

Material thickness directly impacts performance and cost per unit. Standard offerings range from 2mm to 10mm gauge, with 3mm and 4mm representing the most common specifications for general warehouse use. Heavier loads exceeding 2,000 pounds may require 6mm or thicker sheets to prevent flexing during forklift engagement. PP corrugated slip sheets from manufacturers like Elipacko offer FDA-compliant resin options for food-grade applications, with custom sizing available to match specific pallet or container dimensions.

Specification Documentation and Sampling

Most custom orders begin with a technical specification sheet that includes exact dimensions, material grade (virgin vs. recycled content), color requirements, and any printing or branding elements. Wall thickness, flute direction, and corner radius specifications prevent miscommunication during manufacturing. For first-time orders, requesting samples at different gauges allows physical testing before committing to production volumes.

Lead times for custom slip sheets vary by manufacturer location and order complexity. Domestic production typically delivers within 3-4 weeks, while overseas manufacturing requires 8-12 weeks including ocean freight. Elipacko's Thailand and Vietnam facilities operate under 0% anti-dumping duty status for US imports, offering cost advantages over Chinese-sourced alternatives while maintaining comparable lead times. The company is also developing US-based manufacturing capacity to further reduce delivery windows for high-volume customers.

Minimum Order Quantities and Pricing Tiers

Custom slip sheet orders generally require minimum quantities of 500-1,000 units, though this varies by manufacturer and complexity. Simpler rectangular sheets with no printing may have lower minimums, while die-cut shapes or multi-color printing increase both minimums and per-unit costs. Volume pricing typically shows significant breaks at 2,500, 5,000, and 10,000 unit thresholds.

Tooling costs for custom sizes or shapes represent a one-time expense, usually ranging from $200 to $800 depending on die complexity. Some manufacturers waive tooling fees for orders exceeding specific volume commitments. PP divider boards and layer pads can be ordered from the same tooling setup, allowing consolidation of multiple SKUs and improved cost efficiency across your packaging program.

Quality Control and Delivery Logistics

Reputable slip sheet manufacturers provide pre-production samples for approval before full production runs commence. This checkpoint allows verification of dimensions, color matching, and print registration. Quality control specifications should include acceptable tolerances for length, width, thickness, and corner square measurements—typically ±3mm for standard industrial applications.

Delivery logistics require coordination between production completion and warehouse receiving capacity. Slip sheets ship on pallets, typically in bundles of 50-100 units depending on gauge. A full truckload generally contains 8,000-12,000 sheets for 3-4mm material. Partial pallet orders incur additional handling charges but allow smaller facilities to maintain reasonable inventory levels without excessive capital commitment. International orders should account for customs clearance procedures and potential inspection delays, particularly for first-time shipments from new suppliers.

Establishing Ongoing Supply Relationships

After successful completion of initial custom orders, many buyers transition to blanket purchase agreements with scheduled releases. This approach locks in pricing for 12-month periods while allowing flexible delivery timing based on actual consumption rates. Vendors benefit from production planning visibility, while buyers gain protection against material cost volatility and guaranteed capacity allocation during high-demand periods.

Communication protocols should include designated contacts for order placement, quality issues, and expedited requests. Quarterly business reviews help align forecasts with actual usage patterns and identify opportunities for specification optimization or material substitutions that could reduce total cost of ownership. Direct manufacturer relationships eliminate distributor markups while providing access to engineering support for application-specific challenges.
