Slip Sheets as Tier Sheets: A Palletizing and Layer-Stability Guide

Corrugatedslipsheets Editorial • November 2026

Why Tier Sheets Matter in Palletized Loads

Procurement and logistics teams often evaluate slip sheets only as pallet replacements, but the same PP corrugated stock performs a second critical function on the warehouse floor: layer separation. When mixed cases, cans, jars, or bagged goods are stacked in tiers, a thin rigid sheet placed between layers distributes point loads, prevents interlock crushing, and keeps columns square from the bottom pallet to the top course. For operations shipping fragile or irregular SKUs, a well-specified tier sheet is the difference between an intact load and a damage claim at the receiving dock.

How Tier Sheets Stabilize a Load

A corrugated layer pad works mechanically in three ways. First, it bridges the gaps between individual cases so that the course above sits on a continuous plane rather than on the high points of the course below. Second, it increases friction and keys adjacent layers together, which resists the shear forces that develop during transit cornering and braking. Third, a rigid sheet resists the diagonal collapse that unreinforced column stacks are prone to when a pallet is nudged by a forklift or shifts on a trailer deck.

  • Point-load distribution across the full footprint of each layer
  • Shear resistance that limits horizontal layer migration
  • Column squaring that preserves the designed stacking pattern
  • Surface protection for printed or film-wrapped primary packaging

Specifying the Right Gauge

Tier sheet selection follows the same spec logic as any PP corrugated application: match wall thickness and areal weight to the compression load it will carry and the handling it will see. Light consumer-goods layers may only need a 2mm to 3mm sheet, while heavy canned or glass product tiers benefit from 4mm or 5mm twin-wall stock with higher grammage. Because these sheets are reused through many closed-loop cycles, buyers should confirm edge crush resistance and verify that the flute structure will not fatigue after repeated stacking and destacking. Many teams source their layer separation stock alongside PP corrugated slip sheets so that a single material spec covers both pallet-base and tier applications.

Integrating Dividers and Pads

In cells where product must be compartmentalized rather than simply separated by layer, tier sheets work in combination with vertical inserts. Pairing flat layer pads with PP divider boards and layer pads lets a packaging engineer build a complete internal structure that holds bottles, jars, or electronic components in fixed positions throughout the trip. This modular approach keeps the bill of materials lean because the same corrugated family is cut to flat sheets for tiers and to slotted or scored blanks for dividers.

Washdown and Reuse Considerations

One advantage of polypropylene tier sheets over fiberboard is that they survive moisture and washing. In food and beverage plants, layer pads inevitably contact spills and condensation, and PP stock can be rinsed, sanitized, and returned to service without delamination or odor retention. This durability is what makes reusable tier sheets economical: a single sheet that cycles dozens of times costs a fraction per trip compared with disposable fiberboard that is scrapped after one use.

Sizing and Overhang

Tier sheets should match the pallet or load footprint closely. A sheet that is undersized leaves unsupported case edges that can crush, while an oversized sheet creates overhang that snags on racking and stretch-wrap equipment. Most operations standardize tier sheet dimensions to their dominant pallet size and order custom cuts for odd footprints, which keeps inventory simple and ensures consistent load geometry across the facility.

Building a Procurement Spec

When writing a tier sheet specification, document the sheet dimensions, wall thickness, areal weight, target reuse cycles, and any food-contact requirements. Request samples and run them through a realistic stack test before committing to volume. A direct manufacturer relationship shortens this validation loop because custom gauges and sizes can be produced to the exact load profile rather than forcing the operation to adapt to off-the-shelf dimensions.

Conclusion

Corrugated slip sheets are more versatile than their name suggests. Deployed as tier sheets, they stabilize palletized loads, protect primary packaging, and cut transit damage while using the same durable PP material already proven in pallet-replacement roles. For logistics teams chasing lower damage rates and leaner packaging inventories, standardizing on a single reusable corrugated family for both functions is a practical, cost-effective move.
