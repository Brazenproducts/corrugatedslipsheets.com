FDA Food-Grade Compliance for PP Slip Sheets: A Buyer's Checklist

Corrugatedslipsheets Editorial • October 2026

For procurement teams in food and beverage distribution, a slip sheet is not a neutral commodity. Because it sits in direct or near-direct contact with palletized product, it falls under the same food-safety scrutiny as any other packaging material in the supply chain. Specifying the wrong material can trigger a failed audit, a rejected load, or a costly recall. This checklist walks logistics and sourcing managers through what FDA and USDA compliance actually means for PP corrugated slip sheets and how to verify it before you issue a purchase order.

Why Slip Sheet Material Matters for Food Safety

Traditional kraft and wax-coated paperboard slip sheets absorb moisture, harbor microbial growth, and shed fibers. In wet or refrigerated environments, that creates contamination risk and inconsistent performance. Polypropylene slip sheets solve the structural problem, but not every PP sheet on the market is manufactured to food-contact standards. The resin, colorants, and any recycled content all factor into whether a sheet is genuinely food-safe.

This is where sourcing from a manufacturer that controls its resin stream matters. PP corrugated slip sheets produced with virgin, FDA-compliant polypropylene resin give you a documented material pedigree that a generic reseller often cannot provide.

The Compliance Checklist

Before approving a slip sheet supplier, confirm each of the following:

  • FDA 21 CFR 177.1520 compliance. This is the federal regulation covering olefin polymers, including polypropylene, for food contact. Ask for written confirmation that the resin meets this standard.
  • Virgin vs. recycled resin disclosure. Post-consumer recycled content is generally not permitted for direct food contact without specific clearance. Require the supplier to state resin origin.
  • Colorant and additive certification. Pigments and UV stabilizers must also be food-grade. Request documentation for any additives.
  • USDA facility acceptance. For meat and poultry operations under USDA FSIS oversight, confirm the material is acceptable for use in a federally inspected establishment.
  • Lot traceability. A compliant manufacturer can trace resin lots and production runs, which is essential during any audit or investigation.

Documentation You Should Require

Verbal assurances are worthless during an audit. Insist on a letter of compliance or declaration of conformity on manufacturer letterhead, a technical data sheet listing resin grade and additives, and, where relevant, a statement of USDA acceptability. Keep these on file with your HACCP and food-safety records so they are immediately available to auditors and customers.

Matching Material to Application

Compliance requirements tighten as you move closer to open product. A slip sheet under sealed, palletized cases faces different exposure than one in a wash-down zone or in direct contact with produce. Map your applications and document the required grade for each. For operations that also handle divider and layer-pad functions, sourcing coordinated PP divider boards and layer pads from the same food-grade line keeps your documentation consistent across all contact materials.

Sourcing Advantages That Support Compliance

Buying direct from the manufacturer streamlines compliance verification. Elipacko operates as a US-available direct manufacturer with production in Thailand and Vietnam, both of which fall outside current anti-dumping duties, so you avoid the 0% ADD exposure that complicates some import channels. Custom sizing means you can specify sheet dimensions that match your pallet footprint exactly, reducing overhang and the contamination traps that oversized sheets create. Building Compliance Into Your Spec

The most reliable way to protect your operation is to write food-grade requirements directly into your purchasing specification rather than treating them as an afterthought. State the required FDA regulation, resin type, and documentation deliverables in the RFQ itself. Suppliers that cannot meet those terms self-select out early, saving you from discovering gaps during an audit.

Food-grade slip sheet compliance is not complicated, but it does require discipline. Verify the resin, demand the paperwork, match the grade to the application, and source from a manufacturer that can stand behind its material. Do that consistently and your slip sheets become a compliance asset rather than a hidden liability in your food-safety program.
