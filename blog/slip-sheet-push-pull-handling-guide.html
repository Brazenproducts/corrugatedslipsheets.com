Slip Sheet Push-Pull Handling: A Warehouse Operations Guide

Corrugatedslipsheets Editorial • October 2026

Push-pull handling is the backbone of any slip sheet program. When executed correctly, it lets a single operator move unit loads without pallets, cutting both weight and storage cube from your supply chain. When executed poorly, it produces product damage, slow cycle times, and operator frustration that can derail an otherwise sound conversion. This guide walks procurement and operations teams through the fundamentals of push-pull handling with modern PP corrugated slip sheets.

How Push-Pull Attachments Work

A push-pull attachment mounts to a forklift carriage in place of, or alongside, conventional forks. A gripper bar clamps the lip of the slip sheet, and a hydraulic cylinder draws the loaded platen onto the forks. To deposit the load, the platen pushes it off onto a pallet, shelf, or trailer floor while the forklift reverses. The entire sequence takes only a few seconds once operators are trained, and it eliminates the pallet entirely from the handling cycle.

The lip on the slip sheet is critical. A standard single lip of three to four inches gives the gripper bar enough material to clamp without tearing. For heavier unit loads, a reinforced or extended lip distributes clamping force more evenly. This is one of the first specifications to confirm when you order PP corrugated slip sheets, because a lip that is too short or too thin will fail under repeated clamping.

Matching Sheet Gauge to Load Weight

Slip sheet gauge must align with the weight and rigidity of the unit load. Light retail cases can ride on thinner sheets, while dense bagged goods, canned products, or glass require heavier twin-wall stock to resist flexing during the pull stroke. A sheet that bows mid-pull will let the bottom layer of product shift, which is the leading cause of damage in new programs.

  • Light loads (under 1,500 lb): standard-gauge twin-wall PP with a single lip.
  • Medium loads (1,500 to 2,500 lb): heavier-gauge sheet with reinforced lip.
  • Heavy loads (2,500 lb and up): maximum-gauge stock, often with dual lips for bidirectional handling.

Because load profiles vary across SKUs, many operations standardize on two or three sheet gauges rather than a single part. Sourcing from a manufacturer that offers custom sizing lets you match the sheet footprint to your product dimensions instead of forcing product onto an off-the-shelf size.

Forklift Setup and Operator Training

A push-pull attachment reduces a forklift's rated capacity, so verify that your residual capacity still covers your heaviest unit load plus the platen weight. Mast visibility also changes, which is why new operators should practice on empty sheets before handling product. Most teams reach full throughput within a week of structured training.

Key training points include approaching the lip square to the gripper, pulling smoothly rather than jerking, and setting loads down flat to avoid lip tears. Operators should also inspect each sheet before reuse, pulling any with cracked flutes or torn lips out of rotation.

Durability and Reuse

Unlike fiber slip sheets, PP corrugated sheets survive dozens of push-pull cycles, which is where the cost advantage compounds. Pairing slip sheets with PP divider boards and layer pads stabilizes mixed or tall loads during the pull, further reducing damage rates. Together these components let warehouses run a fully reusable, pallet-free handling system.
